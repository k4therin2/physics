# Literature extracts for the fourth-order (one-loop) CMB temperature power spectrum

Target calculation: $C_\ell=\epsilon^2 C^{(11)}_\ell+\epsilon^4\big[\tfrac14 C^{(22)}_\ell+\tfrac13\,{\rm Re}\,C^{(13)}_\ell\big]$, flat radiation-only GR universe, Gaussian $\zeta$, emission on $T_{\rm phys}=T_*$, comoving observer at fixed proper time, normalised by sky monopole, all observer terms kept.

Raw sources (PDF + `pdftotext -layout` text) are in `cmb4/lit/raw/<paper>/paper.{pdf,txt}`. Equation numbers below are those of the arXiv versions downloaded (listed per paper). All equations are transcribed from the extracted text; where the pdf-to-text was ambiguous I checked against neighbouring equations, but spot-check anything load-bearing against the PDF.

Notation warning used throughout: every paper uses conformal time $\eta$ (or $\tau$), signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $\mathcal H=a'/a$, and the Poisson/Newtonian gauge, but they differ in (i) exponential vs linear parametrisation of $\Phi,\Psi$, (ii) the sign/position of the vector mode $\omega_i$, (iii) the factor-of-two convention $X=X^{(1)}+\tfrac12 X^{(2)}$ vs $X=X^{(1)}+X^{(2)}$, (iv) Fourier normalisation, (v) which of $\Phi,\Psi$ is the lapse. These are flagged in each section.

---

## 1. Long-wavelength (constant and gradient) modes, observer terms, CMB consistency relation

### 1.1 Mirbabayi & Zaldarriaga, "CMB anisotropies from a gradient mode", arXiv:1409.4777v2 (JCAP 1503 (2015) 025)
Raw: `raw/mirbabayi_zaldarriaga_1409.4777/`. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.4777

**Conventions.** Poisson gauge with exponentials, eq. (3.1):
$$ds^2=a^2(\eta)\big[-e^{2\Phi}d\eta^2+2\omega_i dx^i d\eta+(e^{-2\Psi}\delta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij})dx^i dx^j\big],\qquad \partial_i\omega_i=0,\ \partial_i\gamma_{ij}=0=\gamma_{ii}.$$
$\omega_i,\gamma_{ij}$ start at second order. Three-velocity $v^i\equiv a e^{-\Psi}u^i$, $u^\mu u_\mu=-1$, $u^0=\sqrt{1+v^2}\,e^{-\Phi}/a$. Matter domination is assumed for all explicit formulas ($a=\eta^2$, $\Phi=\Psi=$const at linear order). Comoving-gauge $\zeta$ defined by $h_{ij}=a^2e^{2\zeta}(e^\gamma)_{ij}$ (Maldacena, eq. (2.2)); in MD $\Phi=-3\zeta/5$ (their footnote 3 and eq. (5.14): $\phi=-\tfrac35\zeta_0$ to all orders for a uniform super-horizon mode). Convention for $f_{NL}$: $\Phi=g-f_{NL}g^2$ (footnote 3). Note their $\zeta$ has the opposite sign relative to $\mathcal R$ of Weinberg/CPV in the sense that $\Phi_{\rm MD}=-3\zeta/5$ here while CPV write $\zeta_L=-5\Phi_L/3$ (same thing); but PUB/SONG use $\Phi=\tfrac23\mathcal R$ in RD with $\mathcal R$ of opposite sign — see Sect. 2.

**Residual gauge freedom in Newtonian gauge at $k=0$ (Weinberg modes), eqs. (2.3)–(2.8):** with linearised metric $ds^2=a^2[-(1+2\Phi)d\eta^2+((1-2\Psi)\delta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij})dx^idx^j]$, the transformations preserving the gauge at zero momentum are
$$\eta\to\eta+\epsilon(\eta),\qquad x^i\to(\delta^i_j+\omega^i_j)x^j,\quad \omega^i_j={\rm const},\tag{2.4}$$
generating
$$\Phi=\epsilon'+\mathcal H\epsilon,\qquad \Psi=-\tfrac13\omega_{ii}-\mathcal H\epsilon,\qquad \gamma_{ij}=\omega_{ij}+\omega_{ji}-\tfrac23\delta_{ij}\omega_{kk}.\tag{2.5}$$
Physicality (extendibility to $k\neq0$, no anisotropic stress) requires $\Phi=\Psi$ (2.6), solved by
$$\omega_{ii}=3C_1,\qquad \epsilon(\eta)=-\frac{C_1}{a^2(\eta)}\int^\eta a^2(\eta')d\eta'+\frac{C_2}{a^2(\eta)},\tag{2.7}$$
$$\Phi=\Psi=C_1\Big(\frac{\mathcal H}{a^2}\int a^2 d\eta-1\Big)\quad(\text{growing mode}),\ \text{corresponding to constant }\zeta=C_1.\tag{2.8}$$
**Gradient mode (2.9)–(2.10):** the zero-momentum coordinate transformation locally equivalent to $\zeta=q\cdot x$ is
$$\eta\to\eta+\epsilon(\eta,x),\qquad x\to(1+q\cdot x)x-\tfrac12x^2q-f(\eta)q,\qquad \epsilon=-f'\,q\cdot x,\tag{2.9}$$
$$f'=\frac1{a^2}\int a^2d\eta,\qquad \Phi=\Psi=(\mathcal Hf'-1)\,q\cdot x.\tag{2.10}$$
(Dilation $(1+q\cdot x)x$ + special conformal transformation $-\tfrac12x^2q$ + time-dependent translation $-f(\eta)q$ + time shift $\epsilon$.) Footnote 4: only the uniform and gradient modes avoid primordial tensors.

**Second-order Sachs–Wolfe formula with observer terms (MD, eqs. (3.2)–(3.7), derived in App. A):**
$$\frac{T(\hat n)}{\bar T}-1=\Theta_e+\Phi_e+\hat n\cdot(v_o-v_e)+I-\delta\hat n\cdot v_e+\hat n\cdot(v_o-v_e)(\Theta_e+\Phi_e)+\Phi_e\Theta_e+\tfrac12\Phi_e^2+(\hat n\cdot(v_o-v_e))^2-\tfrac12(v_o-v_e)^2,\tag{3.2}$$
$$x_e=x_o+(\hat n-v_o+\hat n\,\hat n\cdot v_o)(\eta_o-\eta_e)+2\hat n\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta\,\Phi-2\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta(\eta-\eta_e)\nabla_\perp\Phi,\tag{3.3}$$
$$I(\eta_e,\eta_o)=\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta\big(\Phi'+\Psi'+\omega_i'n^i-\tfrac12\gamma_{ij}'n^in^j\big),\qquad \delta\hat n=-\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta\nabla_\perp(\Phi+\Psi),\quad\nabla_\perp=\nabla-\hat n\hat n\cdot\nabla,\tag{3.4,3.5}$$
$$\Theta(\eta_e,x_e)=\frac{T(\eta_e,x_e)}{\langle T(\eta_e,x)\rangle_x}-1,\qquad \bar T\equiv\frac{a_e}{a_o}e^{-\Phi_o}\langle T(\eta_e,x)\rangle_x.\tag{3.6,3.7}$$
App. A derivation: $T(\hat n)=\frac{E_o}{E_e}T(\eta_e,x_e)$ (A.1), $E=-P_\mu u^\mu$ (A.2),
$$\frac{E_o}{E_e}=\frac{a_e}{a_o}\frac{P_0(\eta_o)}{P_0(\eta_e)}e^{\Phi_e-\Phi_o}\Big(\sqrt{1+v^2}+e^{\Phi+\Psi}v^i\frac{P_i}{P_0}\Big)_o\Big(\sqrt{1+v^2}+e^{\Phi+\Psi}v^i\frac{P_i}{P_0}\Big)_e^{-1},\tag{A.3}$$
geodesic $dP_\mu/d\lambda=\tfrac12\partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}P^\alpha P^\beta$ (A.4); $\frac{1}{P_0}\frac{dP_0}{d\eta}=\Phi'+\Psi'+\omega_i'\tilde n^i-\tfrac12\gamma_{ij}'\tilde n^i\tilde n^j$ (A.5) with aberrated direction $\tilde{\hat n}=\hat n-v_o+\hat n\hat n\cdot v_o$ (A.6); $P_0(\eta_o)/P_0(\eta_e)=\exp I$ (A.8); $P_i(\eta_o)/P_0(\eta_o)=\tilde n^i(1-\Phi_o-\Psi_o)$ (A.11), $P_i(\eta_e)/P_0(\eta_e)=\tilde n^i(1-\Phi_e-\Psi_e)+\delta n^i$ (A.12); full ratio (A.14):
$$\frac{T(\hat n)}{T(\eta_e,x_e)}=\frac{a_e}{a_o}\exp[\Phi_e-\Phi_o+I]\big(\sqrt{1+v_o^2}+\tilde{\hat n}\cdot v_o\big)\big(\sqrt{1+v_e^2}+(\tilde{\hat n}+\delta\hat n)\cdot v_e\big)^{-1}.\tag{A.14}$$

**Normalisation by the observed monopole and the proper-time / constant-temperature slicing (Sect. 3.1):** $\Theta(\hat n)\equiv T_o(\hat n)/\langle T_o(\hat n)\rangle-1$ (3.8). With a super-horizon mean potential $\bar\Phi_e$ over the observable patch: $\langle T_o\rangle=\frac{a_e}{a_o}e^{-\Phi_o}\langle T(\eta_e,x_e)\rangle_{x_e}(1+\bar\Phi_e)$ (3.9), $\langle T(\eta_e,x)\rangle_{x_e}=\langle T(\eta_e,x)\rangle_x(1-2\bar\Phi_e/3)$, hence $\langle T_o\rangle=\bar T(1+\tfrac13\bar\Phi_e)$ (3.10) and $T/\bar T-1=\Theta(\hat n)(1+\tfrac13\bar\Phi_e)+\tfrac13\bar\Phi_e$ (3.11). Fixed observer proper time $t_*$ in MD: $\eta_o=\eta_*(1-\tfrac13\bar\Phi_e)$ (3.12). Constant-temperature emission surface: $\eta_e=\eta_{\rm rec}(1-\tfrac13\Phi_L)$ (3.13) [exponentiated: $\eta_e=\eta_{\rm rec}e^{-\Phi_e/3}$ (5.2)].

**Removal of a super-horizon mode by coordinate change (MD, eqs. (4.1)–(4.10)):**
$$\tilde\eta=(1+\tfrac13\Phi_L(x))\eta,\qquad \tilde y=(1-\tfrac53\Phi_L(x))y+\tfrac56y^2q+\tfrac16\eta^2q,\qquad \Phi_L(x)=\Phi_L(D)+q\cdot y,\ q=\nabla\Phi_L(D),\tag{4.1–4.3}$$
$$\Phi(\eta,x)=\tilde\Phi(\tilde\eta,\tilde x)+\Phi_L,\qquad T(\eta,x)=\tilde T(\tilde\eta,\tilde x),\tag{4.4,4.5}$$
$$\langle T(\eta,x)\rangle=\langle\tilde T(\tilde\eta,x)\rangle e^{2\Phi_L/3},\qquad \Theta(\eta,x)=(\tilde\Theta(\tilde\eta,\tilde x)+1)e^{-2\Phi_L/3}-1,\tag{4.7,4.8}$$
$$v=\tilde v+\tfrac53(\tilde v\cdot q\,y-\tilde v\cdot y\,q)-\tfrac13\eta_e q.\tag{4.10}$$
**Main result (squeezed limit, long mode super-horizon at recombination, corrections $O(\lambda_S^2/\lambda_L^2)$), eqs. (4.11)–(4.15):**
$$\Theta(\hat n)=\Theta_{S,\rm obs}+\Theta_{L,\rm obs}+\Theta_{L,\rm obs}\Theta_{S,\rm obs}+\Delta x_e\cdot\nabla\Theta_{S,\rm obs}-\Delta\hat n\cdot\tilde v,\tag{4.11}$$
$$\Theta_{S,\rm obs}=\tilde\Theta+\tilde\Phi-\hat n\cdot\tilde v,\qquad \Theta_{L,\rm obs}=\tfrac13\Phi_L+\tfrac13\eta_e\hat n\cdot q+\hat n\cdot v_o,\tag{4.12,4.13}$$
$$\Delta\hat n=\delta\hat n+(\hat n\cdot v_o+\tfrac13\eta_e\hat n\cdot q-\tfrac53y\cdot q)\hat n-(v_o+\tfrac13\eta_eq-\tfrac53\hat n\cdot y\,q),\tag{4.14}$$
$$\Delta x_e=\tfrac13\Phi_L\eta_e\hat n+\Delta\eta(-v_o+\hat n\hat n\cdot v_o)+2\hat n\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta\Phi-2\int_{\eta_e}^{\eta_o}d\eta(\eta-\eta_e)\nabla_\perp\Phi-\tfrac53\Phi_Lx+\tfrac56y^2q+\tfrac16q\eta_e^2.\tag{4.15}$$
Short quantities are evaluated at $(\tilde\eta_e,x_o+\hat n(\eta_o-\tilde\eta_e))$ with $\tilde\eta_e=\eta_{\rm rec}$ (constant-temperature slice) — hence no $\partial/\partial\ln\eta_e$ term, unlike CPV eq. (27).

**Pure-gradient consistency check (Sect. 4.1):** with $\Phi_L=\bar\Phi_e+q\cdot x$ the observer acquires $v_o=\tilde v_o-\tfrac13\eta_oq$ (4.21), $x_o=-\tfrac16\eta_o^2q$ (4.22); including (3.11), (3.12) one finds $\Delta x_e\to\Delta x_e-\tfrac13\bar\phi_e\eta_o\hat n-\tfrac16\eta_o^2q=0$ (4.23), $\delta\hat n=-2\Delta\eta(q-q\cdot\hat n\,\hat n)$ (4.24), $\Delta\hat n=0$, $\Theta_{L,\rm obs}=\bar\Phi_e/3$ and finally $\Theta(\hat n)=\Theta_{S,\rm obs}$ (4.25): **a constant plus pure-gradient mode leaves no imprint, including no dipolar power modulation**, once observer position, velocity, proper time and monopole normalisation are consistently included. With a violation of the consistency condition: $\Theta(\hat n)=\Theta_{g_S,\rm obs}(1+\tilde f_{NL}q\cdot x)$, $\tilde f_{NL}=f_{NL}-5(1-n_s)/6$ (4.26–4.27).

**Second order in the long mode (Sect. 5):** $T(\hat n)=\frac{a_{\rm rec}}{a_o}e^{-\Phi_o}T_{\rm rec}\big[1+\hat n\cdot v_o+\tfrac13\Phi_e(1+\hat n\cdot v_o)+\tfrac1{18}\Phi_e^2+I+(\hat n\cdot v_o)^2-\tfrac12v_o^2\big]$ (5.3). Observer proper time with $v_o$ and $\Phi_o$: $dt/d\eta_o=\eta_o^2e^{\Phi_o}\sqrt{1-v_o^2}$ (5.11), giving $t=\tfrac13\eta_o^3(1-\tfrac1{12}q^2\eta_o^2)$ (5.12), $\eta_o=\eta_*(1+q^2\eta_o^2/36)$. Result for $\Phi_0=q\cdot x+\phi_2$:
$$T(\hat n)=T_{\rm CMB}\Big[1-\tfrac5{36}q^2\eta_o^2+\tfrac5{18}(q\cdot x)^2+\tfrac13\phi_2\Big],\qquad x\simeq\hat n\eta_o,\tag{5.13}$$
cancelled by $\phi_2=\tfrac5{12}q^2x^2-\tfrac56(q\cdot x)^2$ (1.8), (5.21) — the second-order adiabatic gradient mode (coordinate transformation (5.15) with $b_1=25/9,\ b_3=-25/36,\ b_4=-25/18$ (5.20)).

**Non-local terms and observer cancellation (App. C):** the non-local pieces of the MD second-order metric (App. B, eqs. (B.1)–(B.4), from Boubekeur et al. 0806.1016; identical to Sect. 5.2 of this file) contribute to monopole and dipole only, $\Theta_0=\tfrac13a_1+\tfrac35a_2\eta_o^2$, $\Theta_1=\eta_ob_3\cdot\hat n+\eta_o^3b_4\cdot\hat n$ (C.14), and are cancelled by (i) the observer proper-time shift $t_*=\int_0^{\eta_o}d\eta\,\eta^2(1+\Phi)=\tfrac13\eta_o^3(1+a_1+\tfrac35a_2\eta_o^2)$ ⇒ $\eta_o=\eta_*(1-\tfrac13a_1-\tfrac15a_2\eta_o^2)$ (C.15–C.16), (ii) the observed mean $\langle T_o\rangle=e^{-\Phi_o}\frac{a_e}{a_o}\langle T(\eta_e,x_e)\rangle$ with $a_e\langle T\rangle=a_{\rm rec}T_{\rm rec}(1+\Theta_0)$ (C.17–C.19), and (iii) the observer velocity $v_o=\tilde v_o-\eta_ob_3+\eta_o^3b_4$ (C.20). The identity $\tfrac13\Phi+I=\tfrac13\phi-\tfrac13\int d\eta\,\eta[2(\hat n\cdot\nabla\phi)^2-(\partial_j\phi)^2]+\Theta_0+\Theta_1+\dots$ (C.1) with explicit non-local $\Theta_0,\Theta_1$ (C.2).

**Reuse:** this is the template for our observer treatment (fixed proper time, monopole normalisation, observer velocity/position induced by long modes). Everything is MD; we must redo (2.7)–(2.10), (3.12), (4.1)–(4.10), (5.11)–(5.12) for RD ($a\propto\eta$, $f'=\eta/3$, $\tilde\eta=(1+\tfrac12\Phi_L)\eta$? — to be rederived: in RD $\Phi=-\tfrac23\zeta$, $\epsilon=-\eta\zeta_L/3$ per CPV eq. (12)).

### 1.2 Creminelli, Pitrou & Vernizzi, "The CMB bispectrum in the squeezed limit", arXiv:1109.1822v2 (JCAP 1111 (2011) 025)

**Conventions.** Linear Newtonian gauge $ds^2=a^2[-(1+2\Phi)d\eta^2+(1-2\Psi)\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j]$ (2); second order with exponentials and $\omega_i,\gamma_{ij}$ (61) exactly as MZ (3.1). $v^i=ae^{-\Psi}u^i$. $\zeta\equiv-\Psi+\mathcal Hv$ in the limit $k_L\to0$ (Weinberg's $\mathcal R$), footnote 2. Effect on short modes (second-order metric in exponential form (15)):
$$\Phi=\Phi_S+\Phi_L+\epsilon\,\partial_\eta\Phi_S+\zeta_Lx^i\partial_i\Phi_S,\quad \Psi=\Psi_S+\Psi_L+\epsilon\,\partial_\eta\Psi_S+\zeta_Lx^i\partial_i\Psi_S,\tag{16,17}$$

Fourier-space second-order transfer functions from this (eqs. (49)–(52), $\Theta=\delta_{\rm rad}/4$):
$$T^{(2)}_\Phi(k_S,\eta)=f(\eta)\frac{\partial T^{(1)}_\Phi}{\partial\ln\eta}-\frac{\partial T^{(1)}_\Phi}{\partial\ln k_S},\qquad T^{(2)}_{\delta_{\rm rad}}=-4f\eta\mathcal H T^{(1)}_{\delta_{\rm rad}}+f\frac{\partial T^{(1)}_{\delta_{\rm rad}}}{\partial\ln\eta}-\frac{\partial T^{(1)}_{\delta_{\rm rad}}}{\partial\ln k_S},$$
with $X^{(1)}_k=T^{(1)}_X\zeta_k$, $X^{(2)}_k=\int\frac{d^3k_Ld^3k_S}{(2\pi)^3}\delta(k-k_L-k_S)T^{(2)}_X\zeta_{k_L}\zeta_{k_S}$ (51–52) — a direct **check of any RD second-order transfer function in the squeezed limit**.

**Squeezed-limit formula (MD, instantaneous recombination), eqs. (23)–(32):** $\Theta_{\rm obs}=[\Theta+\Phi+\hat n\cdot v+\Phi\Theta+\tfrac12\Phi^2+(\Theta+\Phi)\hat n\cdot v](\eta_{\rm rec},x_{\rm rec})$ (23); with $\zeta_L=-5\Phi_L/3$:

(33)–(36)):** a constant $C$ changes the sky average $\langle T\rangle=\bar T(1+C/3)$ (33), rescales short modes by $(1-C/3)$ (34) cancelling the "1" in (27); the recombination-time shift (35) cancels the $\partial/\partial\ln\eta$ term; and $\vec x_{\rm rec}=\hat n(1+\tfrac53C)(\eta_0-\eta_*)$ (36) cancels the $-5\hat n\cdot\nabla$ term. **Full second-order SW with $I^2/2$ (App. A, eqs. (59)–(72)):** $\Theta_{\rm obs}\equiv(T_{\rm obs}-\langle T_{\rm obs}\rangle)/\langle T_{\rm obs}\rangle$ (59); $T_{\rm obs}=\frac{E_{\rm obs}}{E_{\rm rec}}T_{\rm rec}$ (60, exact); (63) $=$ MZ (A.3) with $v_o=0$; (65) $\frac1{P_0}\frac{dP_0}{d\eta}=\Phi'+\Psi'-\omega_i'\hat n^i-\tfrac12\gamma_{ij}'\hat n^i\hat n^j$ [**sign of $\omega$ term opposite to MZ (A.5) because here $\hat n^i=P^i/P^0$, in MZ $\tilde n^i=-P^i/P^0$**]; (70) $\delta n^i=\int d\eta\nabla^i_\perp(\Phi+\Psi)$; result

### 1.4 Weinberg, "Adiabatic modes in cosmology", astro-ph/0302326 (PRD 67 (2003) 123504)
Raw: `raw/weinberg_0302326/`. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0302326

Intermediate identities: $S_1=\frac{(F_{ll_1l_2})^2}{2l+1}$ (54), $S_2=-\tfrac12l(l+1)l_1(l_1+1)\frac{2l_1+1}{4\pi}$ (61), spin-raising (55), $\nabla Y_{lm}=\sqrt{l(l+1)/2}[{}_1Y_{lm}m_+-{}_{-1}Y_{lm}m_-]$ (56), $\sum_m\nabla_iY_{lm}\nabla_jY^*_{lm}=\tfrac12l(l+1)\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}[(m_+)_i(m_-)_j+(m_-)_i(m_+)_j]$ (59), $[(m_+)_i(m_-)_j+(m_-)_i(m_+)_j]\nabla^i\nabla^j{}_sY_{lm}=-[l(l+1)-s^2]{}_sY_{lm}$ (60). **This is the exact template for our $C^{(22)}$ (convolution with $(F_{ll_1l_2})^2/(2l+1)$) and $C^{(13)}$ ($-l(l+1)R\,C_l$) structure**, assuming $\langle\Theta\phi\rangle=0$; in our case the lensing potential is correlated with $\Theta$ (early ISW, SW), so additional $C^{\Theta\phi}$ cross-terms appear in both $(22)$ and $(13)$ (cf. Lewis–Challinor Sect. 4.1 remark and CPV footnote 6).

### 6.2 Lewis & Challinor, "Weak gravitational lensing of the CMB", astro-ph/0601594v4 (Phys. Rept. 429 (2006) 1)
Raw: `raw/lewis_challinor_0601594/`. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0601594
Flat-sky, symmetric convention $\Theta(l)=\int\frac{d^2x}{2\pi}\Theta(x)e^{-il\cdot x}$ (4.1), $\langle\Theta(l)\Theta^*(l')\rangle=C^\Theta_l\delta(l-l')$ (4.6); lensing $\tilde\Theta(x)=\Theta(x+\nabla\psi)$ (4.7);
$$\tilde\Theta(l)\approx\Theta(l)-\int\frac{d^2l'}{2\pi}l'\cdot(l-l')\psi(l-l')\Theta(l')-\tfrac12\int\frac{d^2l_1}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^2l_2}{2\pi}l_1\cdot[l_1+l_2-l]\,l_1\cdot l_2\,\Theta(l_1)\psi(l_2)\psi^*(l_1+l_2-l),\tag{4.9}$$
$$\tilde C^\Theta_l\approx(1-l^2R^\psi)C^\Theta_l+\int\frac{d^2l'}{(2\pi)^2}[l'\cdot(l-l')]^2C^\psi_{|l-l'|}C^\Theta_{l'},\qquad R^\psi\equiv\tfrac12\langle|\nabla\psi|^2\rangle=\frac1{4\pi}\int\frac{dl}{l}l^4C^\psi_l,\tag{4.12,4.13}$$
derived (4.11) as (first-order)$^2$ + 2×(zeroth×second-order) — **exactly the $(22)+(13)$ decomposition** of a one-loop power spectrum. They note the $\langle\Theta\psi\rangle$ correlation is neglected and that the series expansion is not accurate at high $l$ (needs the non-perturbative correlation-function treatment, Sect. 4.2, and full-sky Sect. 9: $\tilde\xi(\beta)=\sum_{lmm'}C^\Theta_ld^l_{mm'}(\beta)\langle Y_{lm}(\alpha_1,\psi_1)Y^*_{lm'}(\alpha_2,\psi_2)\rangle$ (9.4)). Full-sky $O(C^\psi)$ result is Hu's (62) (their ref. (67) = Hu 2000; also Challinor & Lewis astro-ph/0502425).

### 6.3 Fidler, Pettinari, Beneke, Crittenden, Koyama & Wands, "The intrinsic B-mode polarisation of the CMB", arXiv:1401.3296 (JCAP 1407 (2014) 011)
Raw: `raw/fidler_intrinsic_Bmode_1401.3296/`. A worked example of a **purely $(22)$ power spectrum from second-order transfer functions**, (4.1)–(4.9): $a_{X,\ell m}=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)^\ell\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}}\Theta_{X,\ell m}(k)e^{ik\cdot x_0}$ (4.2); $\Theta^{(2)}_{ab}=\tfrac14\tilde\Delta^{(2)}_{ab}+\tfrac12\Theta^{(1)}_{ac}\Theta^{(1)}_{cb}$ (4.3) (brightness→bolometric temperature: factor $\tfrac12=2-\tfrac32$); $C^{BB}_\ell=\tfrac1{16}C^{\tilde B\tilde B}_\ell-\tfrac12C^{\tilde B(IE)}_\ell+C^{(IE)(IE)}_\ell$ (4.5); rotating to $\hat z\parallel k$ via $\tilde\Delta^{(2)}_{B,\ell m}(k)\to\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}}\sum_{m'}Y^{-m'}_{\ell m}(\hat k)\tilde\Delta^{(2)}_{B,\ell m'}(k\hat z)$ (4.7) and $\tilde\Delta^{(2)}_{B,\ell m}(k)=\int\frac{d^3k_1}{(2\pi)^3}\tilde T^{(2)}_{B,\ell m}(k,k_1,k_2)\Phi(k_1)\Phi(k_2)$ (4.8):
$$C^{\tilde B\tilde B}_\ell=\frac{2}{\pi(2\ell+1)}\sum_{m\neq0}\frac12\int dk\,k^2\int\frac{d^3k_1}{(2\pi)^3}P_\Phi(k_1)P_\Phi(k_2)\Big[\tilde T^{(2)}_{B,lm}(k,k_1,k_2)\tilde T^{(2)*}_{B,lm}(k,k_1,k_2)+\tilde T^{(2)}_{B,lm}(k,k_1,k_2)\tilde T^{(2)*}_{B,lm}(k,k_2,k_1)\Big].\tag{4.9}$$
**Reuse:** (4.9) is precisely the full-sky $C^{(22)}$ formula for a second-order transfer function given in the $\hat z\parallel k$ frame (the $m$-sum runs over helicities of the second-order source; for temperature include $m=0$). Their footnote 4: $x_0$ drops out of the power spectrum by statistical homogeneity.

## 7. Existing one-loop / fourth-order CMB temperature power-spectrum calculations

Searches performed (WebSearch, several phrasings: "one-loop CMB power spectrum", "second-order CMB power spectrum", "third-order cosmological perturbations CMB", "intrinsic second order power spectrum", SONG/CosmoLib2nd + power spectrum). Findings:

1. **Tomita, "Second-order power spectra of CMB anisotropies due to primordial random perturbations in flat cosmological models", arXiv:0712.2511v3 (PRD 77 (2008) 103521)** (`raw/tomita_0712.2511/`), and the companion astro-ph/0505157 (`raw/tomita_0505157/`). The only paper found that computes a second-order *power-spectrum* term from products of Gaussian primordial perturbations. Set-up: Einstein–de Sitter (+Λ), dust, **synchronous gauge** second-order metric $\ell^j_i=PL^j_i+P^2M^j_i+QN^{|j}_{|i}+C^j_i$ (2.4)–(2.9), Mollerach–Matarrese second-order temperature formula (2.15)–(2.16); $C_l$ definitions (3.9)–(3.14): $\langle(\delta_1T/T)^2\rangle=T_0^{-2}\sum\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}C_l$, $C_l=T_0^2\int dk\,k^2P_F|H^{(l)}_P|^2$ with $H^{(l)}_P=k(P')_ej_l(kr_e)-(P'')_ej_l(kr_e)+\int d\lambda P'''j_l(kr)$ for $l\ge2$ and observer terms $(P'')_o$, $(P')_o$ kept only in $l=0,1$ (3.11). Second order: $\delta_2T/T$ (3.15), its non-vanishing mean $\langle\delta_2T/T\rangle$ (3.16), (3.21), and the decomposition (3.22)–(3.23)
$$\delta T/T=\delta_1T/T+\langle\delta_2T/T\rangle+\Theta_{pp}+\delta_3T/T,\qquad \langle(\delta T/T)^2\rangle=\langle\Theta^2_p\rangle+(\langle\delta_2T/T\rangle)^2+\langle\Theta^2_{pp}\rangle+2\langle\delta_1T/T\,\delta_3T/T\rangle,$$
**explicitly noting that the $(13)$ term "is of the same order as the second and third terms" but is not computed because third-order metric perturbations were unavailable.** $\langle\Theta^2_{pp}\rangle$ (3.24)–(3.25ff) is the $(22)$ term, evaluated with Legendre/Bessel recursions (3.17)–(3.20). Limitations relative to our target: MD dust only, synchronous gauge, no $(13)$, intrinsic $\tau$ and $A^{(1)}_e$ terms dropped, GW ($C_{ij}$) dropped, observer treated at coordinate time, mean subtracted as ensemble (not sky) mean. Conclusion in the Λ model: second-order ISW power can dominate the (tiny) first-order ISW power at low $l$ in EdS.

2. **D'Amico, Bartolo, Matarrese & Riotto, "CMB temperature anisotropies from third order gravitational perturbations", arXiv:0707.2894v2 (JCAP 0801 (2008) 005)** (`raw/damico_etal_third_order_0707.2894/`): the general third-order geodesic/energy-ratio formula with scalar, vector and tensor modes (Sect. II), large-scale third-order potentials in ΛCDM (Sect. IV); parametrisation $\zeta=\zeta^{(1)}+(a_{NL}-1)\zeta^{(1)2}+(b_{NL}-1)\zeta^{(1)3}$ (1.2), $g_{NL}=\tfrac{25}9(b_{NL}-1)$ (1.3). Oriented to the trispectrum, no power spectrum, observer terms not emphasised. Provides $\Theta^{(3)}$ structure needed for $C^{(13)}$ (in MD/ΛCDM, large scales).

3. **Gao, "On non-linear CMB temperature anisotropy from gravitational perturbations", arXiv:1005.1219** (`raw/gao_third_order_1005.1219/`): third-order SW limit in MD. Non-perturbative large-scale constraint (3.5)–(3.6): $\Psi=\Phi+\partial^{-4}[\tfrac53(\partial^2\Phi)^2+5(\partial_i\partial_j\Phi)^2+\tfrac{20}3\partial_i\Phi\partial_i\partial^2\Phi]$ (third-order part vanishes); $\Phi[\zeta]$ to third order (3.7)–(3.9): $\Phi^{(1)}=-\tfrac35\zeta$, $\Phi^{(2)}=-\tfrac9{25}\partial^{-4}[3(\partial^2\zeta)^2+(\partial_i\partial_j\zeta)^2+4\partial_i\zeta\partial_i\partial^2\zeta]$, $\Phi^{(3)}=-\tfrac{54}{125}\partial^{-4}[3\partial^2\zeta\partial^{-2}+\partial_i\partial_j\zeta\partial_i\partial_j\partial^{-4}+2\partial_i\zeta\partial_i\partial^{-2}+2\partial_i\partial^2\zeta\partial_i\partial^{-4}]\times[3(\partial^2\zeta)^2+(\partial_i\partial_j\zeta)^2+4\partial_i\zeta\partial_i\partial^2\zeta]$; SW: $\Delta T/T=\tfrac\Phi3+\tfrac{\Phi^2}{18}+\tfrac{\Phi^3}{162}\simeq e^{\Phi/3}-1$; kernels (3.12)–(3.15) $\beta(k;p_1,p_2)=-\tfrac1{50}+\tfrac{9(p_1^2-p_2^2)^2}{50k^4}-\tfrac{3(p_1^2+p_2^2)}{25k^2}$, $\gamma=-\tfrac1{125}+[(1-g(p_1,p_{23}))g(p_2,p_3)+2\,{\rm cyc}]$. Full third-order anisotropy with lensing/time-delay/ISW terms (3.10)–(3.11). No power spectrum; MD only; no observer terms.

4. **Fidler, Koyama & Pettinari 1409.2461 App. A** (see 5.2): the redshift–lensing cross term $C^{\rm lensing\text{-}redshift}_l$ — a bona fide fourth-order (one-loop, $(22)$-type with one second-order field being a product) contribution to $C^{TT}_l$, flat sky, numerical, found $10^{-4}$ of the lensing correction. **Fidler et al. 1401.3296**: $(22)$-type $C^{BB}_\ell$ from SONG transfer functions (full sky, eq. (4.9)).

5. **Lensing literature** (Hu 2000, Lewis–Challinor 2006, Challinor–Lewis 2005): the $O(C^\phi)$ lensed $C_l$ is itself the $(22)+(13)$ one-loop structure for the lensing subset of terms; the correlation-function resummation handles all orders in $C^\phi$ but only for lensing.

6. **Not found:** any paper computing the complete $\epsilon^4$ term of the CMB *temperature* power spectrum — i.e. $(22)$ from the full second-order sources (intrinsic SW, Doppler, Rees–Sciama/ISW$^{(2)}$, vectors, tensors, lensing, time delay, local quadratic terms) **plus** $(13)$ from third-order sources — in any gauge, let alone with consistent observer terms, proper-time observer, $T_{\rm phys}=T_*$ emission surface and sky-monopole normalisation. SONG/CosmoLib2nd/Su–Lim–Shellard (1212.6968, `raw/su_lim_shellard_1212.6968/`) compute only bispectra. Searches for "one-loop" in a CMB context return only inflationary loop corrections to $P_\zeta$ (e.g. arXiv:1011.2572, 2504.02609, 2608.03725), which are a different object. Roldan arXiv:1706.08428 (`raw/roldan_nonlinear_SW_1706.08428/`) gives an all-orders, all-gauge non-linear SW+ISW formula (useful for $\Theta^{(3)}$ structure) but no spectra.

**Gap statement for our paper:** (i) no $(13)$ term has ever been computed (Tomita 2008 explicitly flags its absence); (ii) no RD/radiation-only treatment; (iii) no consistent fourth-order observer treatment (all second-order papers drop $\Phi_o$, $v_o$, $x_o$, proper-time shift, except MZ at second order in the long mode); (iv) the only existing $(22)$ temperature computation (Tomita) is MD, synchronous, ISW-only; (v) the closest full-sky harmonic templates are Hu (62)–(63) and Fidler et al. (4.9).
