The CMB temperature spectrum through fourth order

A multi-agent Claude run (about 8 hours of wall-clock with agents in parallel, 16 agent-hours summed sequentially; spread over two days, paused by a usage limit) on a fourth-order CMB problem: “Analytical temperature power spectrum through fourth order”, in a radiation-only GR universe. This page is the summary; everything behind it is in the repo.

For a pure radiation fluid the observed temperature is exactly the local temperature times exp of minus the fluid shear integrated along the ray, which collapses the Sachs–Wolfe / Doppler / lensing / emission-surface bookkeeping into one integral and makes the third-order expansion tractable.

The full fourth-order spectrum (C11, C22 and the previously uncomputed 1×3 term) is assembled with all observer and normalisation terms, evaluated for a monochromatic spectrum, and checked end-to-end against an exact numerical GR solution in a different gauge at first, second and third order.

01 · The physics

What the problem is

The brief asks for the CMB temperature power spectrum Cℓ (how strongly the sky’s temperature varies at each angular scale ℓ) computed analytically through fourth order in the primordial perturbation, in a toy universe that contains only radiation and obeys full general relativity. At fourth order that means not just the textbook first-order term C11, but the product of two second-order maps (C22) and a first-order map times a third-order one (C13, the “1×3” term), with every observer and normalisation term kept.

The usual route tracks Sachs–Wolfe, Doppler, lensing and emission-surface effects separately, and that bookkeeping is what makes third order hard. The run found that for a pure radiation fluid there is an exact shortcut: the temperature seen in direction n is the local fluid temperature at the observer times the exponential of minus the fluid’s shear, integrated along the light ray back to the emission surface. Sachs–Wolfe, ISW, Doppler, lensing, time delay and the location of the emission surface all live inside that one integral, so the observer’s own temperature drops out of the normalised map and the third-order expansion becomes tractable.

Tobs(n) = To · exp[ −S(n) ],   S(n) = ∫eo σμν eμeν dl σ = fluid shear, e = photon direction in the fluid frame, dl = proper length in that frame. Normalised map: 1 + Θ = e−S/⟨e−S⟩sky.

The emission surface enters only as the endpoint of the integral; the observer’s frame enters only as a rotation of the sky, which cannot change Cℓ.

05 · Novelty

Is any of it new?

Honest answer: partly. Two literature searches were run; here is what they found.

Known in general form

The shear formula

A special case of an exact line-of-sight result in Dunsby 1997 (CQG 14, 3391), who wrote it for general matter and then linearised. The explicit exponential-shear form and its corollary (shear-free radiation flow ⇒ isotropic sky for every comoving observer) were not found stated anywhere. The manuscript credits Dunsby.

Appears new · toy model

The fourth-order spectrum

Two literature searches found no complete one-loop CMB temperature spectrum including the 1×3 term (Tomita 2007 explicitly left it out; SONG’s scope excludes it). Appears new, but in a toy radiation-only model.

Reusable

The tools

A sympy Einstein-equation engine to third order; closed forms for every second-order field in radiation domination; a high-precision planar-symmetric GR + radiation solver with ray tracing.

06 · Checks

Verification at a glance

Each link in the chain was checked by something other than the agent that produced it.

  1. Exact shear formula

    the result that organises everything
    • Hostile referee (V1), told to assume it was wrong
    • Kasner spacetime: agrees to 10−31
    • Symbolically, through third order
    • Exact planar GR + radiation solver: residual 1.4×10−14 at Θrms = 0.08

  2. Einstein + fluid engine

    the sympy perturbation engine
    • Bianchi identity to ε3
    • Independent re-derivation by a second referee (V3)
    • Literature controls reproduced: Kohri–Terada tensor source, Lu et al. vector constraint, Pitrou–Uzan–Bernardeau initial conditions
    • Harmonic machinery reproduces the full-sky lensed Cℓ (Hu 2000) exactly

  3. End to end, orders 1–3

    analytic chain vs an exact solver
    • Analytic Θ1, Θ2, Θ3 maps vs the exact planar solver, single plane wave, four configurations, in a different gauge
    • Agreement within the solver’s noise (a20[Θ3] to 7×10−8)

    A single plane wave probes only axisymmetric parts.

  4. C22 assembly

    Monte Carlo (V4)
    • 180,000 realisations of a 20-mode shell field, K2 used as a black box
    • Agrees with the quadrature within 1.1σ at 0.3–0.7% for ℓ = 1–11 (ℓ = 12: 2.0σ)

    Tests the assembly, Wick factors and normalisation, not K2 itself.

  5. The manuscript

    two cold-read referees
    • R1 cold read → W3 referee-driven revision
    • R2 cold read → W4 final editorial pass
